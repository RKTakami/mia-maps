## Cave view, LOD terrain in the world, and a freeze that needed a force-quit

The last published release was **v0.1.12-beta**, so this covers everything since
then. The `v0.1.13-beta` and `v0.1.14-beta` tags exist but were never released;
their contents are folded in here rather than assumed shipped.

Tested on macOS (Apple Silicon) against Mine In Abyss on 1.21.11, on a live server
with Sodium and Iris. **The Windows native is cross-compiled and compile-verified
only — nothing in this release has been run on Windows.** One jar carries both.

> ⚠️ **The freeze fix is not in this jar.** It lives in the companion
> `mia-voxy-rust` build (`...Alpha-5dd6746` or later). This release pairs with it.

---

### Three bugs worth updating for

**The game could hang hard enough to need a force-quit.** The section demoter
sorted resident sections by a field that other threads rewrite constantly, which
makes TimSort throw — and it threw *before* demoting anything, so once the world
was busy enough nothing was ever freed and the cache grew past its budget until
the heap gave out. Fixed in `mia-voxy-rust`.

**The 3D view could freeze on stale terrain.** Voxy's cleaner shuts a world down
when it looks idle and builds a fresh engine on next access — observed about 25
times in one evening's play. Nothing downstream knew, so map tiles, the orbit
cloud and its meshes kept serving data from an engine that no longer existed, and
could not recover while the camera stood still. Every cache is now keyed on which
engine produced it. Separately, the world the player is standing in is no longer
collected as idle at all: that test was a proxy for "the player left", and it only
held while a renderer kept a reference — which on macOS never exists.

**The fullscreen map could open black and stay black.** Its change-detection state
was committed before drawing, so a compose that bailed early requested no tiles,
which meant nothing could ever mark it dirty again.

### Cave view — press V

`RELIEF → VANILLA → CAVES`, in both the 2D map and the 3D view.

Underground the normal map paints the first solid cell below you, which is the rock
you are standing inside — a flat grey wash. Cave view paints only floors, leaving
solid rock dark so passages read against it. Depth is shaded from your own level:
**blue below, red above**, dimming with distance either way. Where the map would be
blank it also looks upward for a ledge, so a wall can show an upper passage without
ever replacing the floor under your feet.

This is deliberately not the see-through-terrain feature removed in `dfeb3e5`. The
2D slice is bounded to 48 blocks and may tunnel only 4 blocks through unbroken
rock; the 3D view keys on **reachability**, so a sealed chamber one cell beyond a
wall stays invisible however close it is. Above ground both are identical to
`RELIEF`. Those bounds are covered by tests.

### LOD terrain drawn into the world (new, off by default)

Settings → **"LOD World Render"**. Draws terrain from the LOD store into the game
world, meshed one stored section at a time.

The point is cost. The 3D orbit view builds a dense grid over its whole view volume
and pays for every cell — solid rock, empty void, everything — so its cost is cubic
in span÷cell and it must pick a coarse level to stay in budget. Measured on this
world: at 512 blocks across, the finest affordable cell is 4 blocks on Low and 2 on
Ultra, which erases a one-block passage by definition.

Working a section at a time changes that shape. Sections the store has never seen
cost nothing. Sections of enclosed rock produce no faces at all. And a section's
mesh is **cached**, so moving the camera re-derives nothing — which, as much as the
cell count, is what forces the dense path coarse. Meshes build on a worker a few at
a time and the view draws what is ready, so terrain fills in progressively rather
than stalling frames.

Depth compositing was verified in game before any of this was built: geometry
submitted through `WorldRenderEvents` is depth-tested against Minecraft's own
terrain, confirmed at Abyss depth 3059 with Iris running.

### 3D view

- **Cutaway (`C`)** — drops everything between the camera and a plane through you.
  Looking down it takes the ceiling off. `[` and `]` slide the plane along the view
  axis and `\` resets it; `R` resets it too. Pinned to the player it could only ever
  slice at your own depth, which is one slice of a 7000-block column at whole-Abyss
  zoom. Implemented as a shader plane, so it cuts a straight edge through geometry
  and keeps the GPU renderer's cascade.
- **Cave Maps (`T`)** — draws terrain translucent and depth-sorted, so cave
  structure inside rock is visible. Off by default; uses the CPU path.
- **Compass rays** pass through terrain and are always visible. They are a reference
  frame anchored on you, and one that vanishes into rock stops telling you which way
  north is exactly when you are buried.
- **Status line** shows mode, GPU or CPU, cutaway state and cut depth.
- The sampled box was 3:1 tall and framed at a third of the viewport; both fixed.
  Over-budget views now coarsen or mesh instead of dropping every Nth surface, which
  used to punch holes in solid ground.
- **LOD Distance Probe** — the depth-compositing experiment, kept as a regression
  test for the render hook.

### Known limits

- **The world renderer submits every quad per frame** through a vertex consumer.
  That is the weak point; vertex buffers are the fix and are not in this release.
- **1-block passages in the 3D orbit view still need a close zoom.** The dense-grid
  ceiling above is unchanged there; the sparse path is a separate renderer.
- Cave Maps above ~85% reads as mush on deep terrain.
- `config/voxy_mia_light_zones.json` containing `"hi"` throws on every boot.
