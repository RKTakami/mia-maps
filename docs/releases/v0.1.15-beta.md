## LOD terrain in the world, and a slidable cutaway

Tested on macOS (Apple Silicon) against Mine In Abyss on 1.21.11, on a live server
with Sodium and Iris. **The Windows native is cross-compiled and compile-verified
only — nothing here has been run on Windows.** One jar carries both platforms.

**This supersedes v0.1.14-beta**, whose tag exists but whose release was never
published. Everything in those notes is included here.

### LOD terrain drawn into the world (new, off by default)

Settings → **"LOD World Render"**. Draws terrain from the LOD store into the game
world, meshed one stored section at a time.

The point is cost. The 3D orbit view builds a dense grid over its whole view
volume and pays for every cell — solid rock, empty void, everything — so its cost
is cubic in span÷cell and it must pick a coarse level to stay in budget. Measured
on this world: at 512 blocks across, the finest affordable cell is 4 blocks on Low
and 2 on Ultra, which erases a one-block passage by definition.

Working a section at a time changes that shape. Sections the store has never seen
cost nothing. Sections of enclosed rock produce no faces at all. And a section's
mesh is **cached**, so moving the camera re-derives nothing — which, as much as
the cell count, is what forces the dense path coarse. Meshes build on a worker a
few at a time and the view draws what is ready, so terrain fills in progressively
rather than stalling frames.

Depth compositing was verified in game before any of this was built: geometry
submitted through `WorldRenderEvents` is depth-tested against Minecraft's own
terrain, confirmed at Abyss depth 3059 with Iris running. `DistanceProbe`
(Settings → "LOD Distance Probe") is that experiment, kept as a regression test
for the render hook.

### Cutaway plane can now be moved

`C` toggles it as before. New: `[` and `]` slide the plane along the view axis and
`\` resets it to the player. `R` resets it too, since R means "back to the player".

Pinned to the player, the plane could only ever slice at your own depth — one
slice of a 7000-block column at whole-Abyss zoom, so most of the model was
unreachable however you turned the camera. The step scales with zoom, because a
fixed step is imperceptible when zoomed out and unusably coarse up close.

### Known limits

- **The world renderer submits every quad per frame** through a vertex consumer.
  That is the weak point; vertex buffers are the fix and are not in this release.
- **1-block passages in the 3D orbit view still need a close zoom.** The dense-grid
  ceiling above is unchanged there; the sparse path is a separate renderer.
- Cave Maps above ~85% reads as mush on deep terrain, and takes the CPU path.
- `config/voxy_mia_light_zones.json` containing `"hi"` throws on every boot.
